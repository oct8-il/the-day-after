quote is optional in the schema. Every cited fixture claim happens to carry one today — 14 of 14 — but the live ledger will not be so tidy, and a drawer that opens onto nothing is worse than a chip that did not open.

So the drawer still opens, still names the source and still gets you out to it. It says the quote is missing rather than pretending the source is.

Slide 5 — the case that never worked

slide 5 · no carousel, and now it does not need one

Slide 5's head carries chips on poll.failure and poll.status — coverage is on for both. It has never had a carousel, and §8 does not give it one: it is the slide where the reader is addressed, not a reading surface.

Under the old ruling those two chips pointed at something that is not on the screen. Nobody noticed because slide 5 has not been built. The drawer closes that quietly — the chip does the same thing on every slide that has one.

This is the argument for the change, beyond preference. A chip that means “look at the strip below” cannot be honoured on a slide with no strip. A chip that means “here is the evidence” can be honoured anywhere.

Behaviour

OpeningTap the chip. The drawer opens directly beneath the block the chip ends — the paragraph, or the list item.
ClosingTap the same chip again, or the × in the drawer's corner. Tapping elsewhere does not close it: on a scrolling column that gesture is a scroll, and the reader would lose the drawer by moving.
One at a timeOpening a drawer closes any other. Two open drawers push the body twice and the reader loses the place they were reading.
Staying in viewIf the drawer would open below the fold, the column scrolls the minimum needed to show it — never more, so the passage above it stays on screen.
Leaving the slideDrawers close. Unlike a stage's scroll position, which §6 now remembers, an open drawer is a question the reader already answered.
The chip, openFills with its type colour at low alpha and takes a border in it, so which chip opened the drawer is never a guess.
Screen readersThe chip becomes a button with aria-expanded and aria-controls; the drawer is a labelled region. On the desktop the chip stays an anchor to the claim in the ledger below — the mark means the same thing, the surface answers differently.

What I would ask you to look at

1 · Does the carousel still earn its place on slide 2? It was load-bearing an hour ago; now it is a strip of the same quotes the drawers hold, in the same order, one tap further from the sentence they belong to. Keeping it is defensible — it is the only view of the sources as a set, and it is how a reader sees breadth without reading the body. But it is now the second way to reach the same thing, and it costs 161px of a column we already found does not fit an iPhone SE.
2 · Slide 4 has no chips to change. You listed slides 2, 3, 4 and 5. §7 says slide 4 carries no sources carousel because “there is nothing to cite for something that has not happened”, and its statement is computed rather than authored — so there is no cite span on that slide and no chip to tap. The ruling can still name slide 4 for completeness, but nothing there will change.
3 · The drawer inherits the +N ordering. §5 orders the carousel by first appearance, deduplicated. A drawer shows the claims of one span in the order the author wrote them in the cite. Those two orders can disagree, and I would rather they did: the drawer answers “what does this sentence rest on”, in the order it was argued.
