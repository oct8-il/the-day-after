3 · Data and schema

The schema, as shipped DIA-365 · DIA-371

Written to the design rather than around it. data/schema/index.ts, abridged to what the deck touches:

export const Summary = z.object({
  stage: STAGE,
  text:  z.string().min(10),          // annotated markdown; the cites live inside the text
});

const Crop = z.object({               // a photo may not ship without its credit
  file, photographer, source, licence, place,
  year: z.string().regex(/^\d{4}$/),
});

export const Photo = z.object({       // two crops, each optional, each falling back alone
  portrait:  Crop.optional(),         // the gate
  landscape: Crop.optional(),         // the slide 6 card
});

export const Poll = z.object({        // the head of slide 5, authored per incident
  question, failure, status, caveat,  // each .min(8)
});

export const Incident = z.object({
  id, parent, he,
  summary:        z.string().min(20), // annotated, and SYSTEMIC — no 7.10 manifestation
  card_line:      z.string().min(8).optional(),
  poll:           Poll.optional(),
  photo:          Photo.optional(),
  feedback_strip: z.boolean().optional(),   // per-item override on the global flag
  claims:         z.array(Claim).min(1),
  summaries:      z.array(Summary).optional(),
});

Two things the earlier draft of this plan predicted and the shipped code does differently: the slide 5 head is one poll object rather than three loose fields, and illustrative is gone rather than carried forward. Summary.lines[].cites[] is gone too — see below.

Annotation, and where the citation guarantee went

The cite array was the schema’s guarantee that no summary sentence is an opinion. Opening the text to markdown put the cite boundary inside the string, where Zod cannot see it, so the guarantee moved rather than lapsed. The contract is docs/annotations.html:

  • A cite span is [[text]](claim-id). lib/annotation.ts parses it; scripts/validate.ts enforces it through checkAnnotated(text, { coverage }).
  • The coverage rule is the guarantee: every assertion is inside a span, and every span resolves to a claim of this incident. It is the one rule --strict gates.
  • coverage is the only thing that varies by field. On: summary, every summaries[].text, poll.failure, poll.status. Off: poll.question and poll.caveat — a question is put to the reader rather than asserted, and a caveat states an absence.
  • Photo fields validate together: a file with no licence fails rather than rendering an uncredited photograph. Dimensions are not checked — the crop is trusted.
  • The two crops are independent. Each falls back on its own — the gate to the number ghost, the card to its bare tint.
Dropped, and worth recording as dropped. v1.3 proposed rejecting any summary that names a date in October 2023, as a cheap proxy for the systemic rule. It was never implemented and should not be: the rule is about what a sentence claims, not which numbers it contains, and a systemic summary may legitimately cite a dated document. The rule lives in the writing and in review.
Still unwritten, and it blocks slides 3 and 4. Taxonomy.stages carries n, he and color and nothing else. The deck asks it for three more per stage — the definition, the absence statement for an unreached stage, and the “who would count as a check” line for stage 5. That is DIA-367. Phases 5 and 6 render placeholders until it lands; nothing else waits on it.

The computed helpers written · 19 Sep

Five rules, pure, with 24 tests. They take the ledger as an argument rather than importing it, so each is testable against a handful of literals; lib/data.ts binds them to the pool the build reads.

Two things the ledger turned out to be that the plan had not noticed. A claim’s source is published as publisher — document (צה"ל — תחקיר הקרב בקיבוץ בארי) in 147 of 205 claims, so splitSource() takes the halves apart and the gate uses the document for its first slot and the publisher for the outlet, exactly as §4 of the spec describes. And ועוד N מקורות counts distinct publishers, not claims: two pieces from כאן חדשות are one source, and counting them twice would be the line lying about its own evidence.

4 · Jump mode (new, unspecced)

Decided in the interview, absent from v2.0 of the spec, and the only hidden gesture on the page. Written here so it can be reviewed before it is built.

  • Arming: press and hold ~400ms on the stage rail (slides 3 and 4) or on the dot row (any slide). The finger stays down for the whole interaction.
  • Moving: slide along the strip. The page scrubs live — each stage or slide renders as it is passed.
  • State: the strip goes to full strength, the page behind drops to ~40%. Both transitions are ~120ms so arming reads as a change, not a glitch.
  • Landing: release. Mode ends with the finger; there is nothing to dismiss.
  • Reach: the rail moves within its own stack only — an unreached rung stays inert on slide 3. The dot row crosses all six slides, slide 4 included.

Jump mode is an accelerator, never the only route. Every stage and slide it can reach is also reachable by ordinary sequential navigation, which is what makes it safe to leave undiscovered — and what makes the page usable with a screen reader, where the gesture does not exist. See §7.

Implementation notes, from the platform rather than the design. A long press on iOS raises the selection callout and the magnifier unless the strip carries -webkit-touch-callout:none, user-select:none and touch-action:none. Arming must cancel the deck's own scroll-snap so the two gestures do not fight. There is no haptics API in Safari, so the feedback is visual or it does not exist. Reduced-motion should drop the scrub to a direct jump on release, keeping the dim.

5 · Build phases

Eight review stops. Seven of them sit between phases and gate the merge; the eighth sits on staging and gates the promotion to prod. The work happens on a fresh branch off dev — dia-360-mobile-item-restart predates the spec rewrite and is dead.

What a gate is, since “review stop” undersells it. npm run dev on the laptop, the phone on the same wifi opening the LAN URL (allowedDevOrigins in next.config.mjs exists for exactly this), the spec’s embedded 390×844 screen open beside it, and the records the gate names — not a general look around. It is not an automated check and it is not a formality: the unit tests, the baselines and the gesture tests all compare the build against something already agreed, and a gate is where that agreement gets made. Phase 9 freezes baselines of whatever the gates accepted, so a screen nobody judged is a mistake about to become permanent. Each one sits at the end of its phase rather than at the end of the build, because a phase’s pattern propagates: gate #3 judges slide 2’s body before slides 3 and 4 inherit it, which is the difference between fixing one screen and fixing three.
Which pool a gate runs on. Fixtures now, live before merge. The instinct is to gate on dev:live, because a gate judges whether the page reads right and that wants real sentences. The live pool cannot carry a gate yet, and the numbers say so plainly: of 33 live incidents, 0 have an annotated summary, 0 a poll, 0 a card_line, 0 a photo, and 2 any stage overview at all. Slide 1 would show 29 number ghosts, slide 2 a body with no source chips, slides 5 and 6 empty heads. The fixture pool is the opposite: six records, every summary annotated, both photo branches, both overview branches, and the slide-4 shapes the live ledger has no example of.
So: the seven merge gates run on npm run dev against data/test, and baselines freeze there too — fixtures are versioned, so a baseline moves only when code moves. Reading the deck on real sentences is a judgement that still has to be made; it just cannot be made first, and until the writing lands a gate on the live pool would only confirm that the fallbacks work.
Gate #8 is a promotion gate, not a merge gate. It was written as a merge blocker waiting on “enough” of the content pass — which is not a date, not a threshold, and not on this plan’s schedule. It would have held the branch open for as long as a few hundred Hebrew strings take to write, and bought nothing: merging is not shipping. The branch merges into dev, and next.config.mjs makes a dev build read data/test — so the merge shows the deck to no reader at all. What exposes it is the promotion to staging and then prod, which is already a decision taken by hand. So the seven gates gate the merge, and the read-through of the real ledger happens on staging, on a phone, before prod — the build that exists to be the site exactly as it would go out.

6 · Verification

Four layers, because pixels alone cannot see a rule and rules alone cannot see a screen.

  1. Unit tests on the computed rules — npm test, node --test over lib/*.test.ts, no runner to install. This is where “reached is the stages claimed, not everything up to the current one” is written down in a form that fails when someone changes it. A baseline says a pixel moved; this says which rule broke.
  2. Frozen 390×844 baselines, on the fixture pool — every slide, every stage page, and the shapes the live ledger cannot show: a one-claim item, an item at the last stage, an item with a portrait and no landscape, an item with no photo at all. Fixtures are versioned, so a baseline only moves when the code moves. The gates are the opposite call and run on dev:live — see §5.
  3. Behavioural assertions, which survive a legitimate redesign: the locator renders at right:14px as measured, not as authored; a rung's y-position is identical on slides 3 and 4; an item at the last stage produces five dots; the stage chip on a card keeps contrast over its photo; the page itself never scrolls.
  4. Gesture tests — Playwright touch emulation for swipe, long-press arming, scrub and release, and the push-once history rule.
The RTL trap, as a test rather than a memory. padding-inline-end and align-items:flex-end resolve to the left in this page, which cost two review rounds. Every edge-anchored element gets an assertion on its measured getBoundingClientRect(), so it can never quietly flip again.

7 · The open questions, answered

Breakpoint decided

The mobile deck renders at (max-width: 599px) and (min-height: 600px). Everything else — tablets, landscape phones, desktops — gets the existing item page.

  • 599px wide covers every phone in portrait, from a 280px folded cover screen to a 430px Pro Max, and stops below the 600–768px band where tablets begin. A 390-wide design stretched to 760 is the thing the whole reimagining exists to avoid.
  • 600px tall is the guard the spec never had. The deck is a fixed-height frame — six slides, a footer on a rule, 26px of home-indicator padding, a vertical rhythm drawn at 844. A landscape phone is 390px tall; the frame cannot survive it, and the desktop page handles that shape today.
  • Height is 100dvh, not 100vh — iOS Safari's collapsing URL bar makes vh taller than the visible viewport, which would push the footer under the chrome exactly where the next-slide label lives.
  • Between 320 and 599px the frame is fluid, not scaled: the 390 baseline sets the proportions, side gutters stay 20px, and type does not shrink. A scaled 390 canvas would blur text on a 430 screen.

Where the ratings go moved to DIA-376

Version A ships, so the MVP has nothing to send and this question leaves the critical path. The work below is drafted rather than discarded — it is carried in DIA-376, along with the per-item override, and should be decided when the strip is actually built rather than now.

The draft answer was Supabase, called from the client. A single insert-only table — feedback( id, item_id, rating int 1..5, created_at, device_key ), RLS letting anon INSERT and not SELECT. It keeps output: 'export' intact, it is the same instance the full slide 5 would need for public answers and the ballot, and device_key is a random localStorage id — enough to stop one phone rating twenty times, not an identity. The anon key is public by nature and the table is therefore spammable; acceptable for developer feedback, not acceptable for a ballot, which would need a real gate first.

Also moved: whether the strip shows on every item. The answer stands — a global site.feedbackStrip with a per-item feedback_strip override, which the schema already carries — and the reasoning stands with it: the strip asks about the site, not the failure, so the item is not really the axis, and it costs a card. A reader swiping through several failures should not see four cards, then three, then four, for no reason they can perceive. The override is for the exception; making it the mechanism is what would make the slide look unstable.

Share decided

Web Share API where it exists — on iOS and Android that is the native sheet, which is the whole point on a page reached from Instagram. Where it does not, the fallback copies the item link, not the deep link: /item/i13/ with no hash, so a shared link always opens at the gate. A short toast confirms the copy.

Keyboard and screen reader decided

The question was whether the deck has any route through it that is not a gesture — on a phone, that means VoiceOver and TalkBack, where swipe means "next element", not "next slide". The answer is that nothing is gesture-only:

  • The deck is a scroll container of six <section>s, each labelled in Hebrew (השער, סקירת הכשל, …). A screen reader moves through them in DOM order; scroll-snap follows focus, so the visible slide and the spoken one stay together.
  • Slides 3 and 4 are the same shape one level down — a labelled section per stage page.
  • The footer's previous/next labels are real links to the slide hashes, so they work as ordinary navigation for anyone who never swipes.
  • A hardware keyboard gets arrow keys: left/right across slides, up/down within a stack. Cheap to add, and it is how the Playwright gesture tests will drive the deck.
  • Jump mode has no keyboard or AT equivalent, and needs none — it reaches nothing that sequential navigation does not.
  • prefers-reduced-motion drops the live scrub to a direct jump on release, and the deck's slide transitions to instant. The dim stays: it is state, not motion.

Stale social posts closed

Nothing is live; the five posts get regenerated from the new numbering. The generator reads the same computed number as the site, so the two cannot drift again.

Desktop page on a phone closed

Not a goal. No link, no escape hatch, no "view desktop version". A phone under the breakpoint gets the deck.

The two left open

How long the gate’s document slot may be new, 19 Sep

Writing sourceLine against the real ledger turned up a fit problem the mock could not show. The spec’s example line is תחקיר צה״ל · כאן חדשות · ועוד 2 מקורות — 38 characters. Measured across the 29 published items, the real lines run 11 to 75 characters, and 11 of 29 are over 46, which is roughly what fits on one line at 11.5px in the gate’s column. The worst is סיכום דוח צוות המומחים לבדיקת תחקירי צה"ל · Times of Israel · ועוד 3 מקורות.

Naming the publisher instead of the document brings that same line to 43 and every line under the limit — but then the slot no longer names a document, which is the thing that makes the gate’s attribution worth reading. The helper returns both (document and documentPublisher) so the decision stays open and lives in one place. Four ways it could go: keep the document and let the component truncate with an ellipsis; keep the document and drop to the publisher only when it would overflow; use the publisher always; or treat the long ones as a data problem and shorten those source strings.
