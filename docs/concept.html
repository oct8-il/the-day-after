Thesis

Israel has produced an unusual volume of documentation about the failures of October 7 — IDF probes, State Comptroller audits, Knesset hearings, a civil commission, years of investigative journalism. What does not exist is a single place where a citizen can see, for each documented failure, what has actually changed since — and say whether they believe it.

היום שאחרי is that place. It is a structured, sourced, continuously updated ledger. Every entry is a failure someone credible documented; attached to it is every subsequent claim of acknowledgement, planning, implementation or independent verification — each with a link, a date and a source type. Beside the evidence sits a second, deliberately separate signal: how many verified Israelis believe the failure has been fixed.

The project takes no position on blame. It takes one position on facts: a claim without a public source does not exist here.

It is

  • A ledger of documented failures and documented follow-up.
  • A collection of the sources and testimonies that expose each failure — drawn on a map where they have a place.
  • One public question per failure, matched to its stage, kept in its own chapter apart from the evidence.
  • A public methodology with a visible corrections log.

It is not

  • A verdict on who is responsible.
  • A memorial, an archive of testimony, or a news site.
  • A place where votes decide what is true.
  • A poll about trust in institutions or the media — polarising questions are out of scope by design.
  • A publisher of anything that has not already cleared publication elsewhere.

Purpose, audience, stance

Purpose

Three goals, in priority order. Accountability pressure: make it hard for an institution to leave a documented failure quietly unfixed — "1,096 days later, still at stage 2" is a sentence the site should be able to produce automatically. Civic education: let an ordinary reader see the systemic picture rather than the headline of the week. Public memory: a durable, well-sourced record that is hard to rewrite because every line points somewhere.

Audience

The general Israeli public, Hebrew-first. Journalists, researchers and decision-makers are welcome second-order users — the data model is built so they can cite it — but the reading experience is designed for someone who has ten minutes and a phone.

Stance

Descriptive on facts, neutral on blame, explicit about method. The project never says "X is to blame". It does say "source A documented this failure; source B says it was acknowledged; source C says it was implemented; nobody independent has verified it yet". Where the project exercises judgment — the lifecycle stage an item sits at — the rule producing that judgment is published and mechanical.

The credibility rule. Anyone from any political camp should be able to read the methodology page and fail to find a lever. If a critic can point at a rule and say "this is where your bias lives", the rule is wrong, not the critic.

Scope

Four domains in v1. The fourth is deliberately narrowed.

Civilian systems & home frontYesPolice readiness and coordination; MDA / ZAKA / hospitals; Home Front Command alerts; community readiness squads (כיתות כוננות); evacuee handling; rebuilding the envelope.

The narrowing of the fourth domain is a credibility decision, not an evasion, and the methodology page should say so plainly: the project tracks mechanisms that failed and whether they were repaired. Judging pre-war policy is a legitimate task for others.

Rules

Compute

An item's stage is the highest stage for which at least one claim exists. Nobody has to agree for this to be calculated. "The IDF says it is implemented" moves the item to stage 4 — that is a fact about what the IDF said.

Independence

Stage 5 requires a source whose type is independent — State Comptroller, Knesset committee, court, or an investigative report with named documentary basis. The implementer can never verify itself.

Contest

"Disputed" is a flag, not a stage. A contesting claim attaches to the specific claim it disputes. The item shows a contested badge and the contesting link; the stage does not move down. Politics can add a link; it cannot stall the ledger.

Regress

Stage 6 is the one exception to monotonicity: a sourced report of erosion or reversal sets the item to Regressed and preserves the prior peak stage in history. A later implementation claim moves it back up.

No URL, no claim

Every claim carries a URL to an already-published source, a date, and a source type. A claim missing any of the three is not entered. This is the entire anti-politics mechanism, and it is meant to be boring.

Public opinion, one question per stage

Opinion lives here and nowhere else. The public does not set the stage; it rates what the current stage offers. Because a plan and an implementation are different things, the question changes as the item moves.

Chapters

When an item advances a stage, the open question closes and its results are frozen as a chapter. The item page shows the current question with its live distribution, then the earlier chapters underneath: "the public rated the plan 2.1 of 5 (1,870 verified); after implementation, 3.4 (4,820 verified)". A vote can be changed while its chapter is open. Parents do not average scores across chapters — the questions differ — they show the stage distribution of their incidents instead.

Before the answer: the arguments

The voter sees the arguments before the buttons. Two rows, never blended. From published sources: what analysts, family organisations and investigative reports have said for and against the plan or implementation — each a claim with a link. From the public: the free-text explanations voters left, clustered automatically into a handful of for/against arguments. Each cluster states how many answers it summarises and can be opened to show two or three representative quotes the editor approved. There is no raw comment feed: clustering keeps the moderation load survivable for one person and removes the defamation surface.

Free text is optional, anonymous, and enters a summarisation queue. The AI never invents an argument that is not in the answers; the editor reviews clusters before they publish, exactly as with claims.

Decision: no ID upload to the project, at any stage. Holding scans of תעודות זהות would be the single largest liability in the project (Israel's Privacy Protection Law, strengthened by the 2024 amendment in force since August 2025, treats such a database as sensitive with registration, DPO and breach duties), would suppress participation on the most loaded topic in the country, and would not stop a determined actor anyway. Identity assurance, when wanted, is bought from a provider that keeps the document.

Display rules

  • Always show the breakdown openly: "12,400 votes · 9,100 phone-verified". Transparency about imperfection beats pretending to have solved Sybil resistance.
  • No numbers shown below a minimum (say 50 phone-verified votes) — a percentage of 7 people is noise dressed as data.
  • The headline figure is the phone-verified share; signed-in-only votes are shown but labelled.
  • The single most important view in the product is the gap: items at stage 4–5 whose implementation the public rates below 2.5 of 5. That is where the accountability story is, and it is a headline neither an institution nor a mob can manufacture alone.

Editorial policy

Curation

One curator — Roy — approves every entry and every claim. An automated daily scan (Hebrew news feeds, Knesset committee agendas and protocols, State Comptroller publications, IDF Spokesperson) matches new material against open items and proposes claims into a review queue. Nothing publishes unreviewed. The bottleneck is deliberate at this stage; it buys a consistent standard while the rules bed in.

Naming

Institutions, units and systems only: "Gaza Division HQ", "Shin Bet–AMAN coordination", "Sderot police station", "the alert system". No individual commanders, officials or ministers, even when a source names them. Quotes are trimmed to remove names.

One scoped exception. The analysts' table in chapter 3 names the authors of published analysis — a researcher, a columnist, an NGO's report author — with their affiliation, because an argument without an author is not a source. It never names anyone involved in the events. In the prototype these are invented names paired with generic institution descriptors, never invented people attached to real named institutions.

Victims and survivors

No names of victims or survivors. No graphic detail beyond what is necessary to state the failure. No testimony quotes unless already published with the speaker's evident consent, and then only as a linked claim, never as narrative.

Only already-public sources

The project publishes nothing that has not already appeared in a source that carries its own responsibility for clearance. Every claim links to its original. This sidesteps the military-censor question by design and keeps the project out of the business of breaking news.

Summaries

Plan and implementation summaries are drafted by AI from the claims, approved by the editor, and footnoted sentence by sentence. The summary is a convenience; the source is the authority, and the label on every summary says so.

Methodology and corrections

A public methodology page states the lifecycle rules, the per-stage questions, source types, naming and privacy policy, and the verification tiers in plain Hebrew. A corrections log lists every stage change, reversal and error with date and reason. Anyone can submit a correction or a candidate source through a form; submissions enter the same review queue as the automated scan.

Product

Hebrew, right-to-left, mobile-first. Visual at the top, textual as you drill down.

Entry: the intro page

A first visit opens on על הפרויקט: the name, the one-line thesis, and a single column of prose — why the site exists, what you are about to see on the failures page, what a failure drill-down contains, what the project will not do, who is behind it — with the full method folded underneath as a collapsible section and a call to action into הכשלים. After that first visit the site opens on the failures page and the intro stays reachable as a deliberately secondary tab: smaller, muted, after a separator.

Home: the failure matrix

The systemic failures laid out on two axes: who (military & intelligence · home front & civilian systems · society & institutions) across, when (before · the day itself · after) down. Each failure is a tile filled with its average stage's colour, with an icon in a tinted chip, a two- or three-word label, a stage-distribution bar and "x of y implemented" in its footer. Empty cells stay visible as dashed slots — the shape of the space is information too. Tapping a tile opens a drawer with the incidents; that is where text begins.

A strip above the matrix carries the numbers that must be true before anything else: days since October 7; items tracked, and how many systemic failures they group into; a six-cell stage ladder — the count in each stage, in order, coloured, with נסוג set apart after a dashed divider as the exit rung, no legend; and the gap — how many items are reported implemented while the public rates the implementation below 2.5.

The opening sequence

On the first visit to the failures page only, the strip is the first and only thing on screen: centred, enlarged, fading in while every number in it counts up from zero over the same 1.8 seconds. It holds, then settles into its place at the top of the page. Only then does the matrix reveal itself, row by row, top to bottom, with a fade. The sequence runs once (a local flag), never under prefers-reduced-motion, and leaves no inline styles behind.

The map, demoted

There is no map page. A map appears inside an item, as an extension of "what failed", when the item's evidence has places: every testimony and place-specific report is a pin coloured by source type, so a hotline collapse pins to every town a resident was quoted from. Items whose evidence has no place — a commission of inquiry, an assessment culture — have no map. The map is a way of seeing the source collection; the collection is the thing.

Item page: one story in three chapters

A single column, read top to bottom, in numbered chapters separated by a hairline with a coloured tick. A docked header (title, stage chip, back) follows the reader below the site header; on wide screens a fixed chapter rail with scroll-spy sits beside the text, and on narrower ones a tinted chapter line in the dock replaces it.

Analysts are a face-off table: a central spine with בעד and נגד headers carrying claim and writer counts, note-style bubbles stretched to their columns so both edges align down the page, monogram avatars on the outer flanks with a popover listing every writer and affiliation. The public is a WhatsApp-style thread on a recessed panel, three lanes: supportive bubbles on one side, critical on the other, and mixed answers centred, dashed and tailless, because they belong to neither side. Verbatim answers carry their rating as a chip ("rated 4 of 5"). Bubble tones are a softened sage and clay so stance reads as tone, not as a traffic light; the saturated pro/con colours stay on the yes/partial/no bar and legends.

The stage question is restated directly above the 1–5 scale so nobody rates without it in view. Results are review-style horizontal rows beside a large mean. Past chapters are derived, not stored: every stage the failure actually reached gets a row, and a stage that advanced without a voting window says so instead of vanishing.

The page closes with a single band: actions (submit a source, share, how the stage is computed) beside the parent's other incidents. The share card (Open Graph image) is generated per item and carries the title, the stage, and the current question's result — that image is the social launch.

Other views

The gap view (stage 3–5 on one axis, the public's 1–5 rating of the plan or implementation on the other; the highlighted zone is "reported implemented, rated low"). The corrections page. The method lives inside the intro page. A browse-by-place view and a timeline are explicitly deferred.

Visual language

Visual at the top level, textual as you drill down. Colour encodes exactly one thing per surface.

Stage colours are an ordered ramp

The six stages are one cool-to-green ramp — slate, blue, cyan, teal, green — with lightness and saturation rising with the stage, so "further along" reads without a legend. נסוג is the only warm hue, a rust, because it is an exit, not a rung. The ramp survives a deuteranopia simulation where the earlier amber/green pairing collapsed into one olive.

Amber is emphasis only

The accent is reserved for calls to action and for the gap number, which makes the gap the single amber thing on the failures page — exactly where the accountability story is. No stage uses it.

Contest is a flag, not a state

Legal & risk

A civilian project on this subject will be attacked on method, on law, and on motive. Most of the defence is design, done before launch.

Defamation claim by an individualInstitutions-only naming; quotes trimmed of names; pro-bono legal review before launch.Medium
Military censorship exposureOnly already-published sources, always linked. The project originates nothing.Low
Privacy law (voter data)No ID documents ever. Phone hashed. Minimal retention. Written privacy notice. Check registration duties with counsel once user counts are known.Medium

MVP & five-week plan

Live on 7 October 2026. Small, sourced, and honest about what it does not yet cover.

In the MVP

  • ~8 systemic parents, 25–40 incidents, every claim with URL, date and type. Seeded from the IDF's published probes (February 2025 wave), State Comptroller and Knesset committee reports, the Civil Commission of Inquiry, and press investigations.
  • Matrix home with headline strip; item pages with evidence list, evidence map, stage summary and ledger; gap view; methodology and corrections pages.
  • Per-stage questions with Google/Apple sign-in and Israeli phone OTP; thresholds and tier labels; free text collected. Argument clusters from published sources at launch; public clusters once enough answers exist to cluster honestly.
  • Share cards per item. Hebrew only.

Not in the MVP

Identity-verified tier, English, timeline view, open contributor accounts, the automated monitor (manual scanning until the item set is stable — the monitor is week 6+).

Open questions

  1. The eight parents. Which systemic failures make the cut for v1? Proposed starting set: early warning not acted on; border defence architecture; Gaza Division command and readiness; reinforcement and response time; police coordination; emergency medicine and evacuation; alert and communication systems; community readiness squads. Government-process parents (hostage apparatus, NSC procedures) may wait for v1.1.
  2. Map source. The schematic SVG is the working default; real tiles inside the item page only if the schematic proves insufficient with real evidence.
  3. Analyst naming. The scoped exception (authors of published analysis are named) should get a line in the pre-launch legal review.
  4. Chapter transitions. When a stage advances, does the new question open immediately with zero votes, or after the stage summary is approved? Proposed: after the summary — voters should have something to rate.
  5. Source-side arguments. Which source types may appear in the "from published sources" argument row — research and civil society only, or press commentary too? Proposed: research, civil society, and investigative press with documentary basis; no opinion columns.
  6. Vote threshold. The minimum number of phone-verified votes before a percentage is shown. 50 is a placeholder.
  7. Contest weight. Does a contesting claim from an independent oversight source (not just civil society) do anything beyond flagging? Proposed: still a flag — but the flag's colour reflects the contesting source type.
  8. Domain and handle. Candidates around oct8 / 8october / השמונה; check availability across .il, .org.il, and the social platforms in week 1.
  9. Who reads the method before launch. Names of two or three credible readers from different camps, and one family organisation contact.
  10. What "day zero" looks like publicly. Whether to state on the home page that the dataset is partial and invite sources, or to present only what is complete.
